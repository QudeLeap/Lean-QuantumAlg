Polynomial-time integer factorization (Ekerå–Håstad)

Theorem statement

Let $0<\eta<1$ and $k=\lceil\log(\eta/2)/\log(147/256)\rceil$. For all sufficiently large integers $n$, set $\ell=\lfloor(n+2)/2\rfloor$ and suppose $(p,q)$ is drawn uniformly from the ordered pairs of distinct primes satisfying $2^{n-1}<p,q<2^n$, with known $N=pq$ satisfying $2^{2n-1}<N<2^{2n}$. Given access to oracles $V_{N,g,x}$ satisfying $V_{N,g,x}\ket{a,b,y} =\ket{a,b,y\oplus(g^a x^{-b}\bmod N)}$ for units $g,x$ modulo $N$, there exists a quantum algorithm that returns a factor $d\in\{p,q\}$ with probability at least $1-\eta$, using at most $3n+2\ell+1$ logical qubits, $16k$ queries to $V_{N,g,x}$, $16k\left(n^2+2n\ell+2\ell^2+n +3\lfloor(n+\ell+1)/2\rfloor+3\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor\right)$ CNOT gates, $k(24n^2+48n\ell+48\ell^2+107n+98\ell+49)-3n-2\ell-1$ single-qubit gates, $k\left(40(n+\ell)^2+56(n+\ell)+113\right)-1$ quantum computation time, $880803934n+293601328\ell+587202961$ classical bits, and $k(2^{52}n+1)$ classical operations.
