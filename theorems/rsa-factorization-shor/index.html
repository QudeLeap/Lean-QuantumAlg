Polynomial-time integer factorization (Shor)

Theorem statement

Suppose $p$ and $q$ are unknown distinct primes and $N=pq$ is known. Let $n=\lceil\log_2N\rceil$, $0<\eta\leq1$, and $k=\lceil\log\eta/\log(1-1/(48n))\rceil$. Given access to modular-exponentiation oracles $U_{N,a}$ satisfying $U_{N,a}\ket{z,y} =\ket{z,y\oplus(a^z\bmod N)}$, there exists a quantum algorithm that returns a factor $d\in\{p,q\}$ with probability at least $1-\eta$, using at most $3n$ logical qubits, $k$ queries to $U_{N,a}$, $k(4n^2+n)$ CNOT gates, $k(6n^2+4n)$ single-qubit gates, $3n+k(66n^2+232n+317)+2$ quantum computation time, $k(22n+1)+70n+42$ classical bits, and $k(56n^2+222n+316)+2$ classical operations.
